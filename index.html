Ad Account Morning Check

One command. Three answers every Meta + Shopify operator needs every morning before they touch anything.

Yesterday's real contribution margin. Day-over-day trend. Paused ads that spent >$50 last week.

Why this exists

Meta tells you ROAS. Shopify tells you revenue. Neither tells you whether you actually made money yesterday.

Every morning, most media buyers waste 10-15 minutes bouncing between Meta Ads Manager and Shopify Admin to assemble the same three answers. Then they make decisions on Meta's reported ROAS — which doesn't subtract COGS, shipping, payment processing, or returns.

This skill does the real math: starts from Shopify cash collected, subtracts your actual unit-economics costs, then subtracts ad spend. The number that comes out is what hit the bank after paying everything ad-related.

What you get

Section 1

Yesterday's real CM. Revenue, COGS, shipping, processing, returns reserve, ad spend, then the number that matters: contribution margin in dollars and percent.

Section 2

5-day trend. Side-by-side daily CMs so today's number has context. Catches drift, weekday vs weekend patterns, and the days where pre-ad margin tanked.

Section 3

Paused-with-spend audit. Every ad that spent >$50 in the last 7 days that's currently paused. The "good ads stranded" list. One-glance recovery.

Why contribution margin and not ROAS?

A Meta-reported 2.0x ROAS sounds profitable. But:

  • COGS typically 30-40% of revenue
  • Shipping ~5-10% (your real cost, not what you charge)
  • Payment processing ~3-4%
  • Returns reserve ~2%
  • Ad spend already in the ROAS denominator

At 2.0x ROAS with typical unit economics, contribution margin is often 5-8% — barely above zero. The May 15 example above shows it: 2.09x ROAS, 7.1% CM. Scale at "2x ROAS" and you scale near break-even.

This skill makes you face the real number every morning, so the first decision of the day is grounded in actuals — not Meta's reported revenue.
